Mechanical Design · FEA · AI/ML

Physics-Informed Gaussian Process Modeling & Bayesian Optimization of a Robotic Gripper Mechanism

Lead-screw-driven gripper for a fishnet-grabber application, optimized with FEA-trained Gaussian-process surrogates and Bayesian optimization, then prototyped and tested.

  • SolidWorks
  • ANSYS
  • Python
  • GP / BO
  • 3D printing

Engineering problem

Design a gripper (shaft, arm and body assembly) for the CFT Pyramid Fishnet Grabber that grips reliably and stays mechanically stable — while keeping the number of expensive finite element simulations needed to explore the design space manageable.

Methodology

  • Lead-screw actuation converts motor rotation into controlled linear opening/closing of the arms.
  • Parametric SolidWorks model; design of experiments with Lloyd-optimized Latin hypercube sampling.
  • ANSYS structural FEA on each sample; mesh-sensitivity checks.
  • Gaussian-process surrogates trained on physics-derived dimensionless features, with robust outlier screening.
  • Bayesian optimization (expected improvement with feasibility constraints) to propose new designs, verified back in FEA.
  • 3D-printed prototype with an integrated motor-control circuit; experimental gripping tests.

Physics-informed Gaussian-process surrogates for robotic gripper design

  • Three-component gripper assembly (shaft, arm, body) parameterized in CAD and sampled with Latin hypercube DOE.
  • ANSYS structural analyses generate training data; physics-derived features reduce multicollinearity and improve GP kernel fitting.

Research methodology

  1. 01

    Parametric CAD

    Design variables defined in SolidWorks.

  2. 02

    Design of experiments

    Space-filling Latin hypercube samples.

  3. 03

    FEA

    ANSYS structural runs with mesh checks.

  4. 04

    Physics features

    Dimensionless geometric ratios as model inputs.

  5. 05

    GP surrogate

    Kernel selection, uncertainty estimates, outlier screening.

  6. 06

    Bayesian optimization

    Constrained expected improvement proposes new designs.

Iterative loop — proposed designs are evaluated in FEA, added to the training set, and the surrogate is refit until convergence; final designs are prototyped and tested.
